# Abrasions

## Immediate steps
- Wash your hands if possible, then irrigate the wound thoroughly with clean running water or sterile saline.
- Continue rinsing until no dirt or foreign material is visible in the wound.
- Remove easily accessible debris with clean tweezers if necessary.
- Cover a clean abrasion with a clean, non-stick dressing and change it when it becomes wet or dirty.

## Do not
- Do not aggressively scrub the wound.
- Do not blow on the wound.
- Do not leave visible dirt or debris embedded in the wound.

## Seek emergency help when
- Dirt or foreign material cannot be fully removed.
- The wound was caused by a human or animal bite.
- Redness, swelling, increasing pain, foul-smelling drainage, or fever develops.
- The wound is deep, extensive, or associated with significant bleeding.
- Tetanus vaccination may not be up to date, especially for a deep or dirty wound.
