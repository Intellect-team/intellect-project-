# Severe Bleeding

## Immediate steps
- Apply firm, direct pressure on the wound with a clean cloth or bandage.
- Raise the injured area above heart level if possible, without moving a suspected fracture.
- If blood soaks through the cloth, add more layers on top rather than removing the original one.
- Once bleeding visibly slows, clean gently and cover with a sterile dressing.

## Do not
- Do not remove a cloth that has soaked through — layer over it instead.
- Do not remove an embedded object; apply pressure around it instead.
- Do not use a tourniquet unless trained to do so and bleeding is truly life-threatening and uncontrolled.

## Seek emergency help when
- Bleeding does not stop after 10 minutes of continuous firm pressure.
- Blood is spurting (possible arterial bleeding) or the wound is very deep.
- The person shows signs of shock: pale/clammy skin, rapid breathing, confusion, weakness.
