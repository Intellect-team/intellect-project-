## Signs
- A bruise occurs when small blood vessels under the skin break and blood collects beneath the skin
- The area may appear black, blue, purple, or another dark color
- The color may change as the bruise heals
- Swelling, tenderness, or pain may occur

## Do
- Elevate the bruised area above heart level if possible
- Apply an ice pack wrapped in a thin towel for about 20 minutes at a time
- Repeat cold application several times during the first day or two
- If swelling occurs, a light elastic bandage may be used, provided it is not tight
- If the skin is intact, a bandage is generally not necessary
- Use an appropriate nonprescription pain reliever if normally safe for the person and according to the product instructions

## Don't
- Don't apply ice directly to the skin
- Don't wrap an elastic bandage tightly
- Don't ignore severe or unexplained bruising

## Seek medical care when
- The bruised area has very painful swelling
- A lump develops over the bruise
- Bruising occurs frequently or involves unusually large or painful bruises
- Bruises appear suddenly or without an obvious injury
- There is a personal or family history of easy bruising or bleeding
- There is unusual bleeding, such as bleeding from the nose or gums
- Pain persists after a minor injury
- The bruising is associated with a significant injury or suspected fracture

Sources: Mayo Clinic (Bruise: First aid), IFRC International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025 (General first-aid assessment and care principles), 2024 American Heart Association / American Red Cross Guidelines for First Aid (General first-aid principles)
