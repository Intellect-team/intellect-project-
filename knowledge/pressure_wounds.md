# Pressure wounds

## What it is
- A pressure wound (pressure ulcer/injury) is localized damage to the skin and underlying tissue caused by sustained pressure, often together with shear
- Common locations include the heels, sacrum, hips, elbows, and other areas over bony prominences
- Risk increases with limited mobility, reduced sensation, inability to reposition, nutritional problems, moisture, and previous pressure ulcers

## Signs
- Persistent redness or discoloration over an area exposed to pressure
- Skin that is warmer, firmer, softer, or more painful than surrounding skin
- Non-blanching redness or discoloration
- A blister or shallow open wound
- Deeper tissue damage with exposed tissue in more severe injuries
- Black or brown necrotic tissue may occur in severe wounds

## Do
- Relieve pressure from the affected area
- Reposition the person according to their individualized care plan
- Use appropriate pressure-redistributing equipment when indicated
- Inspect the skin regularly, particularly over bony areas
- Keep the skin clean and manage excessive moisture
- Maintain adequate nutrition and hydration according to the person's clinical needs
- Have an existing pressure ulcer assessed by an appropriately trained healthcare professional
- Follow the prescribed wound-care and dressing plan

## Don't
- Don't massage or rub an area of suspected pressure damage
- Don't continue applying pressure to the affected area
- Don't use ordinary cushions or mattresses as a substitute for appropriate pressure-redistributing equipment when such equipment is required
- Don't routinely apply antiseptics or topical antimicrobials to a pressure ulcer unless specifically indicated
- Don't attempt sharp debridement unless appropriately trained and authorized to perform it

## Seek medical care when
- A pressure wound develops or is worsening
- There is spreading redness, warmth, swelling, or increasing pain
- There is pus, foul-smelling drainage, or other evidence of infection
- The person develops fever or signs of systemic infection
- There is exposed deeper tissue, extensive necrosis, or suspected bone involvement
- The wound is located on the heel or another high-risk area and pressure cannot be adequately relieved

## Professional management
- Pressure ulcers should be assessed and classified using a validated system
- Wound size, depth, and undermining should be documented when appropriate
- Pressure redistribution and repositioning are central components of management
- Nutritional assessment should be considered
- Debridement may be required depending on the amount of necrotic tissue and the wound's characteristics
- Dressing selection should consider wound location, exudate, pain, and dressing-change frequency

Sources: NICE CG179 (Pressure ulcers: prevention and management), NICE (Pressure ulcer prevention and management recommendations), international pressure injury classification/guidance
