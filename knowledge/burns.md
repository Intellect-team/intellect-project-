## Signs
- Burns may cause redness, pain, swelling, or tenderness
- Partial-thickness burns may produce blisters and moist or damaged skin
- Full-thickness burns may appear dry, leathery, white, brown, or black and may have reduced sensation
- Severe burns may be associated with rapid swelling or smoke-inhalation symptoms

## Do (thermal burns)
- Make sure the area is safe and move away from the heat source if possible
- Cool the burn immediately with clean, cool running water
- Continue cooling for at least 10 minutes and ideally about 20 minutes
- Remove rings, watches, belts, and other tight items near the burn before swelling develops
- After cooling, loosely cover the burn with a clean, non-stick dressing or clean cloth
- For small burns that can be managed at home, appropriate options after cooling may include petroleum jelly or aloe vera
- Monitor the person for signs of shock if the burn is severe

## Do (chemical burns)
- Protect yourself from the chemical
- Remove contaminated clothing or jewelry if safe
- Brush off dry chemicals before rinsing when appropriate
- Rinse the affected area thoroughly with running water
- Seek emergency medical advice for significant chemical burns, especially those involving the eyes, face, hands, or genitals

## Don't
- Don't deliberately use ice or ice-cold water as the routine method of cooling
- Don't pop or remove blisters
- Don't pull clothing that is stuck to burned skin
- Don't apply butter, oil, or other household substances to the burn
- Don't attempt to clean or treat a major burn beyond appropriate first aid while waiting for professional care

## Seek emergency medical care when
- The burn is full-thickness or appears dry, leathery, white, brown, or black
- A partial-thickness burn is larger than the person's palm
- The burn involves the face, hands, feet, or genitals
- The burn involves a major joint
- The burn wraps around an arm, leg, or other body part
- There is rapid swelling
- The burn was caused by electricity or lightning
- The burn was caused by a strong chemical
- There are signs of smoke or inhalation injury, such as facial burns, difficulty breathing, singed nasal hairs, or soot around the nose or mouth
- The person shows signs of shock

Sources: 2024 American Heart Association / American Red Cross Guidelines for First Aid (Cooling Thermal Burns), IFRC International First Aid, Resuscitation and Education Guidelines 2025 (Burns), Mayo Clinic (Burns: First aid), Mayo Clinic (Chemical burns: First aid)
