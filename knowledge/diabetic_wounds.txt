## What it is
- A diabetic wound is a wound or area of damaged skin that occurs in a person with diabetes and may be harder to heal
- Diabetes can affect blood circulation, sensation, and the body's ability to fight infection, which can make some wounds heal more slowly
- Diabetic wounds can occur on different parts of the body. A diabetic foot ulcer is one important type
- A wound may cause little or no pain when diabetes-related nerve damage reduces sensation

## Signs
- A wound, open sore, cut, blister, or area of broken skin that heals slowly
- Redness, swelling, warmth, or tenderness around the wound
- Drainage, pus, or an unpleasant smell
- Skin discoloration or tissue that appears dark or black
- Reduced sensation or numbness around the affected area
- Increasing wound size or failure to improve

## Do
- Keep the wound clean and protected with an appropriate clean dressing
- Monitor the wound regularly for changes
- Follow the person's prescribed diabetes treatment and blood-glucose management plan
- Seek professional medical assessment, especially if the wound is not healing normally
- For a wound on the foot, reduce pressure on the affected area and seek medical assessment promptly

## Don't
- Don't ignore a wound simply because it is not painful
- Don't remove dead or damaged tissue yourself
- Don't apply antibiotics, antiseptics, or other medical products unless advised by a healthcare professional
- Don't walk on or repeatedly put pressure on a foot wound unless a healthcare professional has advised that it is safe

## Seek medical care when
- The wound is not healing or is getting larger
- Redness, warmth, swelling, increasing pain, or drainage develops
- There is pus, a bad smell, or other signs of infection
- The skin becomes dark, black, or unusually discolored
- There is fever, chills, or the person feels generally unwell
- The wound is deep, extensive, or exposes deeper tissue
- A person with diabetes develops a new wound on the foot, particularly if there is reduced sensation or circulation

## Professional assessment
- A healthcare professional may assess the wound itself, blood circulation, sensation, infection, and factors affecting healing
- Diabetic foot wounds may require specific assessment of neuropathy, peripheral arterial disease, infection, and pressure/offloading
- Treatment depends on the wound's cause, location, severity, and the person's overall condition

Sources: International Working Group on the Diabetic Foot (IWGDF) 2023 Guidelines (Classification, Infection, Offloading, PAD, and Wound Healing), NICE NG19 (Diabetic foot problems: prevention and management), IWGDF Definitions and Criteria 2023
