# Cuts and Abrasions

## Immediate steps
- Clean your hands before touching the wound if at all possible.
- Rinse the cut gently with clean running water to remove dirt or debris.
- Apply gentle pressure with a clean cloth if there is minor bleeding.
- Cover with a sterile adhesive bandage or dressing once clean and dry.

## Do not
- Do not use alcohol or hydrogen peroxide directly in a deep wound.
- Do not remove a large or deeply embedded object from a wound.
- Do not ignore a wound that shows signs of infection (increasing redness, warmth, pus, worsening pain).

## Seek emergency help when
- The cut is deep, gaping, or you can see fat, muscle, or bone.
- Bleeding does not slow after several minutes of firm pressure (see bleeding.md).
- The wound was caused by a rusty or dirty object and the person's tetanus vaccination is not up to date.
- There is numbness, or the injury is near a joint, the face, or a major blood vessel.
